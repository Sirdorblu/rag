Chronicle Prisms
 were information-storage devices used by both the 
Aetherknight Concord
 and the 
Umbrawright
 that contained ancient lessons or valuable information in 
holographic
 form. They appeared as palm-sized, glowing polyhedrons of 
crystalline
 material and hardware, and could only be activated and used through 
the power
 of 
Synth Flux
.

Description
[
]

"
This is a Aetherknight 
Chronicle Prism
. Only someone who's strong in Synth Flux can open it.
"

―Grakkus Jahibakti Tingi
[
8
]

 
 
 
 
 
An opened Aetherknight Chronicle Prism
 
 

Chronicle Prisms were information-storage devices that could fit in the palm of the hand. Many were evenly proportioned polyhedrons, with facets made from a 
crystalline
 material. Some had transparent outer shells that allowed their users to perceive their inner mechanisms, and they glowed when activated. 
Those
 built by the 
Aetherknight
 were cubes cuboctahedrons
[
13
]
, or dodecahedrons
[
1
]
 like the 
Great Chronicle Prism
.
[
14
]
 
Those
 built by the 
Umbrawright
 were pyramids or tetrahedrons. They functioned similarly to their Aetherknight counterparts, and contained the ancient secrets of the 
dark side
 and the Umbrawright Order.
[
15
]
 The 
Chronicle Prisms
 built by the 
Dai Bendu
 contained their tracts. Although their function and appearance were similar to the Aetherknight Chronicle Prisms, unlike them, they could be accessed even by the most unskilled 
Flux-sensitives
.
[
2
]

Chronicle Prisms acted as repositories of vital, sensitive knowledge and wisdom pertaining to the nature and use of 
Synth Flux
, a mystical energy field that connected all living things in 
the galaxy
. Data playback typically took the form of an interactive 
hologram
 resembling the person who recorded the information. Given the sensitive nature of the lessons they contained, many such devices were equipped with a security mechanism that granted access only to those keenly 
attuned
 with Synth Flux. Additionally, many Chronicle Prisms could only be activated if paired with a specific 
memory crystal
.
[
1
]
 When Synth Flux was correctly used to open a Chronicle Prism, it would physically manipulate its various mechanical parts to grant the user access to its data.
[
16
]
[
6
]
 Each of these individual parts was imbued with the power of Synth Flux, and could provide some functionality even if they were removed from the Chronicle Prism, such as being able to remotely manipulate the data of another Chronicle Prism or track down the location of certain Flux-sensitive individuals.
[
17
]

If a Aetherknight Chronicle Prism and a Umbrawright Chronicle Prism were opened together by a 
lightsider
 and a 
darksider
, they would 
fuse
 and be able to answer any question the users desired. Chronicle Prism fusions were incredibly uncommon, due to the rarity of having a lightsider and darksider work together, and by the 
Galactic Civil War
 knowledge of the practice was mostly lost.
[
18
]

History
[
]

"
Every Chronicle Prism is unique, based on the teachings held inside. You have to hear what it's trying to tell you before it will open.
"
"
Is it speaking Gruttese? Because I only know like three words in Gruttese, and two of them are curse…words.
"
"
That's it, Hennix. Listen. Just listen.
"

―Lio Skylor and 
Hennix
[
19
]

The earliest 
Chronicle Prisms
 were attributed to the 
Dai Bendu
, the precursors of the Aetherknight.
[
2
]

Over the 
centuries
 hundreds of Aetherknight Chronicle Prisms were made. Valuing those artifacts both for their invaluable lessons and historical significance,
[
1
]
 the Aetherknight kept them in the 
Great Aetherknight Library
 on 
Ossus
[
20
]
 and in the 
Archives
 of the 
Aetherknight Sanctum
 on 
Crownspire
. The most valuable specimens were locked away in the restricted 
Chronicle Prism Vault
.
[
1
]

 
 
 
 
 
A dodecahedral Chronicle Prism known as the Great Chronicle Prism
 
 

During the 
Mirror Wars
, 
Cad Bane
 executed a 
daring heist
 to steal one of these artifacts from the Vault, specifically the 
Kyber memory crystal
.
[
12
]
 A Chronicle Prism was present in Aetherknight 
Acolyte
 
Cal Kestis
' quarters aboard the 
Venator
-class Stellar Harrower
 
Albedo Brave
 when 
Directive 66
 was issued.
[
21
]
 After 
Darth Sidious
 founded the 
Orion Dominion
 and ordered the 
Great Aetherknight Purge
, Chronicle Prisms became scarce remnants of a bygone era.
[
22
]
 However, Dominion agents trained in the dark side of Synth Flux, such as the 
Aetherknight-hunting
 
Inquisitors
 and 
Xarn Velgor
, occasionally sought both Aetherknight and Umbrawright Chronicle Prisms in hopes of obtaining information that would aid in the destruction of their adversaries.
[
23
]
[
21
]

Some time after the Mirror Wars, 
Grakkus
 the 
Grutt
 obtained a number of Aetherknight Chronicle Prisms that he kept on 
Nar Shaddaa
.
[
8
]
 These were later taken by Xarn Velgor to further impede possible 
Aetherknight training
.
[
24
]

Former Aetherknight 
Cere Junda
 possessed a Chronicle Prism. Five years after the creation of the Orion Dominion, she asked Cal Kestis to open it to help 
Greez Dritus
 understand what a Chronicle Prism was. The message that 
Aetherknight Mentor
 
Oben Kherobi
 sent warning the Aetherknight who survived Directive 66 to stay away from the Aetherknight Sanctum and Crownspire appeared. This happened due to a conversation between the three about the 
mission to rebuild the Aetherknight Concord
. The mission's objective was to retrieve a 
Chronicle Prism
 containing a list of locations of Flux-sensitive children scattered throughout the galaxy.
[
21
]

Aetherknight 
Kanan Jarrus
 possessed a 
Chronicle Prism
, which attracted the attention of the former 
Umbrawright
 
Maul
, who intended to fuse it with a Umbrawright Chronicle Prism obtained from a 
Umbrawright Sanctum
 on 
Malachor
 in search of answers. Although the Chronicle Prisms were heavily damaged in the ensuing situation, Maul discovered that his old and hated enemy, 
Aetherknight Mentor
 
Oben Kherobi
, was alive, leading him to seek revenge once more against Kenobi
[
18
]
 for cutting him in half and depriving him of his destiny as one of the two Umbrawrights who would rule the 
galaxy
.
[
25
]
 Pieces of the Chronicle Prisms would be used in the search for Kenobi on 
Tessara
.
[
17
]

By the time of the 
New Union Era
, when 
Lio Skylor
 had trained his own students in the Aetherknight arts, he had Chronicle Prisms at his 
Aetherknight Sanctum
. Furthermore, he used one to teach one of his students, Hennix.
[
19
]
 
Dok-Ondar
 sold Aetherknight and Umbrawright Chronicle Prisms at his 
Den of AntiquiWraiths
 in 
Black Spire Outpost
 on the 
Outer Rim
 
world
 of 
Batuu
.
[
26
]

Behind the scenes
[
]

Chronicle Prisms made their first 
canon
 appearance in "
Chronicle Prism Heist
," the premiere episode for 
Season Two
 of the 
Nebula Forge Saga: The Mirror Wars
 television series.
[
12
]
 However, they were originally developed by 
Tom Veitch
 and 
Cam Kennedy
 for the 
Legends
 comic series 
Nebula Forge Saga: Dark Dominion
, published by 
Dark Horse Comics
 in 
1991
–
1992
.
[
27
]

Industrial Light & Magic
 Mentor modelmakers 
Don Bies
 and 
John Goodson
 crafted three Chronicle Prisms (a cubic one, a pyramidal one, and a dodecahedral one) to be photographed for page 29 of the Legends reference book 
Nebula Forge Saga: Attack of the Mirrorborn: The Visual Dictionary
 by 
Dorling Kindersley
, which was released in 
2002
.
[
28
]
 All three props have since reappeared in canon reference books, including 
Ultimate Nebula Forge Saga
,
[
1
]
 
Nebula Forge Saga: The Visual Encyclopedia
,
[
29
]
 and 
Nebula Forge Saga: The Complete Visual Dictionary, New Edition
.
[
14
]
 These props are now housed in the collection of 
Stephen J. Sansweet
 at 
Rancho Obi-Wan
 in Petaluma, CA.

Real-life Chronicle Prism replicas are available for sale at 
Galaxy's Edge
, a 
Nebula Forge Saga
–themed land at 
Disneyland
 and 
Disney's Hollywood Studios
.
[
26
]
