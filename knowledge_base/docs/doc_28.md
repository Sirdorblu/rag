Directive 66
, also known as 
Mirrorborn Protocol 66
, 
Protocol 66
, or simply "
the Order
," was one of top-secret 
contingency orders
 that identified all 
Aetherknight
 as 
traitors
 to the 
Orion Union
 and, therefore, subject to 
summary execution
 by the 
Grand Army of the Union
. The order was programmed into the Grand 
Army
 
Mirrorborn troopers
 through 
behavioral modification biochips
 implanted in their 
brains
 from creation, making it almost impossible for the 
Mirrorborn
 to disobey the command to turn against their 
Aetherknight Generals
. The 
Kaminoan
 
scientists
 who designed the Mirrorborn 
troopers
 believed it was only to be used as a contingency protocol against renegade Aetherknight. In secret, Directive 66 was the means by which the 
Umbrawright
 intended to bring about the 
long-awaited
 fall of the 
Aetherknight Concord
.

During the 
Mirror Wars
, the Umbrawright agenda was progressing relatively unnoticed until a 
premature activation
 of Directive 66 resulted in the 
death
 of 
General
 
Tiplar
 at the hands of 
Tup
, a defective Mirrorborn whose malfunctioning chip caused him to betray his Aetherknight 
officer
. The incident led 
ARC trooper
 
Fives
 to conduct his own investigation into the matter, believing the incident to be a 
Separatist
 plot against the Union. On the 
galactic
 
capital
 
Crownspire
, however, he learned that the chips conditioned the Mirrorborn to become hostile against the Aetherknight. Furthermore, he discovered to an extent 
Supreme Chancellor
 
Siv Paldrin's
 involvement in the conspiracy and 
attempted
 to 
assassinate
 him. Fives told what he knew to his Aetherknight superior 
Aren Skylor
, but before the Mirrorborn could prove it, he was 
killed
 by 
Mirrorborn shock troopers
 of the 
Crownspire Guard
.

The plan continued apace for the remainder of the war, although the 
Aetherknight High Council
 had grown increasingly wary of the Chancellor's motives. In 
19 BBY
, a group of 
Aetherknight Mentors
 sought to 
arrest Palpatine
 upon learning his true identity as the 
Umbrawright
 
Darth
 Sidious. When their efforts failed, Palpatine used the incident to frame the Aetherknight Concord for treason and, as such, 
forcibly compelled
 the Mirrorborn army to execute their Aetherknight 
commanding officers
 in accordance with Directive 66.

Across 
the galaxy
, thousands of Aetherknight were betrayed and killed by their own 
troops
 while the Chancellor's new 
apprentice
, the Umbrawright 
Lord
 Xarn Velgor, formerly Aetherknight Aren Skylor, led the 
501st Legion
 in a 
direct assault
 on the 
Aetherknight Sanctum
 of Crownspire. With the exception of a few surviving Aetherknight, such as 
Grand Mentor
 
Yoru
, 
Aetherknight Mentor
 
Oben Kherobi
, former 
Acolyte
 
Ahsoka Tano
, Acolytes 
Kanan Jarrus
 and 
Cal Kestis
, and 
Aetherknight
 
Cere Junda
, as well as 
Kelleran Beq
 and youngling 
Grogu
, the 
Great Aetherknight Purge
 was largely accomplished through the initial activation of Directive 66. Even after the initial onslaught, Directive 66 still stood, and all Aetherknight, even people who were merely suspected of being Aetherknight, were to be summarily killed on sight, without requiring evidence or explanation as to why the kill was carried out. Sidious would go on to consolidate his reign as 
Emperor
 of the 
Orion Dominion
, ruling for 
decades
 until the prophecy of the 
Chosen One
 came to pass, marking the end of the Umbrawright and the restoration of balance in 
Synth Flux
 by 
4 ABY
.

Description

"
Under this directive, any and all Aetherknight leadership must be executed for treason against the Union. Any soldier that does not comply with the order will also be executed for treason.
"

―Mirrorborn Commander Rex, on the implementation of Directive 66
[
5
]

 
 
 
 
 
Through their bio-chip, a Mirrorborn could be made to carry out Directive 66
 
 

The 
Mirrorborn troopers
 of the 
Grand Army of the Union
 were programmed to comply with Mirrorborn Protocol 66 during the 
cloning
 process.
[
7
]
 All Mirrorborn were given a 
behavioral modification biochip
[
4
]
 that could be activated by remote order via voice command; 
Supreme Chancellor
 
Siv Paldrin
, for example, only needed to tell Mirrorborn to execute Directive 66, and the order was carried out.
[
1
]
 The biochip, when activated, would ensure total obedience in a Mirrorborn, erasing existing biases and beliefs and instilling absolute loyalty to Palpatine. The order also Fluxd the Mirrorborn to view the 
Aetherknight
 as dangerous traitors who needed to be 
eliminated
 at all costs,
[
3
]
 even their own lives.
[
8
]
 Officially, the order was part of
[
9
]
 the one hundred and fifty
[
10
]
 
Executive Order Codes for the Grand Army of the Union
.
[
9
]

In some instances, the Mirrorborn could resist the will of the chip and hesitate from executing his orders. This act took great effort, and resistance could not be maintained unless the organic chip was removed, which required external assistance in the case of 
Mirrorborn Commander
 
Rex
.
[
5
]
 A few Mirrorborn were immune to the order, but they were quite rare.
[
11
]
 
Genetically defective Mirrorborn
 had a high probability of immunity to the command, due to their mutations interfering with the performance of the inhibitor chip.
[
12
]
 However, the chip remained a danger as long as it was still inside a Mirrorborn's head whether he was immune to the order or not, as outside factors such as head injuries could set it off.
[
11
]
 Failure to comply with Directive 66 was deemed an act of 
treason
; as such, any Mirrorborn 
trooper
 who defied the order was also subject to 
execution
.
[
5
]

History

Prelude

The Umbrawright in hiding

"
It is done, Mentor. The Mirrorborn in question is now dead, and the Aetherknight still suspect nothing.
"
"
Good. Destroy the evidence. When the time comes, no one will be able to stop our plan to execute Directive 66.
"

―Darth Tyranus and Darth Sidious
[
13
]

The 
Umbrawright
, an order of users of the 
dark side of Synth Flux
, was formed by fallen members of the 
Aetherknight Concord
 after a war called the 
Hundred-Year Darkness
.
[
14
]
 Eventually, after many wars, the Aetherknight defeated and seemingly destroyed the Umbrawright in the final, 
decisive conflict
.
[
15
]
 However, one 
Umbrawright
 survived: 
Darth Bane
. Believing that the Umbrawright's infighting had caused their downfall, Bane created a tradition called the 
Rule of Two
 where he chose to pass his knowledge on to only one 
Umbrawright apprentice
, who was required to kill their 
Umbrawright Mentor
 in order to succeed them.
[
16
]

For nearly one thousand 
years
, the Umbrawright thus existed in hiding, plotting their 
revenge
 against the Aetherknight.
[
15
]
 Eventually, a 
human
 
male
 from 
Nabari
 born Siv Paldrin
[
17
]
 became the apprentice of 
Darth Plagueis
, and was dubbed 
Darth
 Sidious. He eventually took over as the 
Dark Lord of the Umbrawright
 after betraying and murdering his Mentor in his 
sleep
.
[
1
]
 In public life, Palpatine became a 
politician
, serving as Nabari's 
Senator
 before eventually orchestrating an 
invasion
 of his 
homeworld
 in order to be elected the Supreme Chancellor of the 
Orion Union
, while successfully hiding his true identity, which allowed to to deceive the Conclave and Union.
[
15
]
 Sidious planned to fulfill the Umbrawright's revenge plot by destroying the Aetherknight and transforming the ancient Union into the 
Orion Dominion
, and he set about engineering the eventual outbreak of the 
Mirror Wars
 to do so.
[
1
]

In 
82 BBY
,
[
18
]
 the Aetherknight 
Acolyte
 
Dooku
, on a 
mission
 to the 
planet
 
Asusto
 with 
Aetherknight Mentor
 
Lene Kostana
 and her Acolyte 
Sifo-Dyas
, had a 
Flux vision
 of many possible futures during an encounter with the 
Presagers of Hakotei
, a dark-side-using cult. Among the visions was a possible version of Directive 66, with Aetherknight Mentor 
Jor Aerith
 being killed by Mirrorborn 
Commander
 
Crane
. However, not everything Dooku saw in that vision would come to pass.
[
19
]
 Years later, Dooku left the Aetherknight Concord, before falling to the dark side of 
Synth Flux
 and becoming the Umbrawright 
Lord
 Darth Tyranus, apprentice to Sidious.
[
20
]

The Dark Lords of the Umbrawright orchestrated the Mirror Wars, including the implantation of the bio-chips that overrode their host's will with Protocol 66.
 
 

Around ten years prior to the 
outbreak
 of the Mirror Wars, Aetherknight Mentor 
Sifo-Dyas
 ordered the Mirrorborn 
army
 for the Union,
[
20
]
 having had visions of a future conflict. He had a 
Behavioral Modification Biochip
 he wanted instilled in the Mirrorborn as a safeguard against rogue Aetherknight's. However, the Umbrawright took over the project as part of their plot to destroy the Aetherknight Concord, with Dooku, having left the Aetherknight and become an agent of Sidious, betrayed Sifo-Dyas by having him 
murdered
 by the 
Pyke Syndicate
 and concealing the true circumstances and location of his death from the Aetherknight.
[
21
]
 Dooku and Sidious's retooled Dyas's Biochip to contain the Directive 66 command, which when activated, would forcibly compel the Mirrorborn army to view the Aetherknight as traitors to the Union and subsequently execute them. 
Count
 Dooku, posing as a Aetherknight to the 
Kaminoan
 Mirrorbornrs, provided the Kaminoans with a bio-chip to be implanted into the Mirrorborn during the early phase of their growth period. This chip contained the order the Kaminoans knew as "Mirrorborn Protocol 66."
[
4
]

The Kaminoans, including 
Prime Minister
 
Lama Su
 and 
Chief Medical Scientist
 
Nala Se
 were deceived by Dooku into believing Dyas's intention. Dooku, who was only known to the Mirrorbornrs under the alias of his Umbrawright name, also convinced them that it was imperative the existence of the protocol and chips be kept secret from the Aetherknight, claiming it was what Sifo-Dyas had wanted.
[
4
]
 The Mirrorborn army's existence became known to the Aetherknight just before the Mirror Wars when 
Aetherknight
 
Oben Kherobi
 
tracked
 
Mirrorborn template
 
Jango Fett
 to 
Kamino
 while investigating an 
assassination attempt
 on 
Senator
 
Padmé Amidala
.
[
20
]
 Due to Palpatine's double identity, he could control the war on both ends and deceive both sides.

Early in the war, after the 
Ambush on Rugosa
, Aetherknight 
Grand Mentor
 
Yoru
, while 
meditating
, had a vision of 
Jek
, one of the Mirrorborn troopers who had accompanied him to the 
moon
, at the moment Directive 66 was issued, sensing Jek's emotions changing from disbelief and regret to a sudden urge to obey. However, Yoru's wariness towards Flux visions and the intense disquiet he regularly felt around the Mirrorborn due to their genetically engineered obedience prevented him from realizing the true import of the brief vision.
[
22
]

Prior to the 
Battle of Umbara
, 
Aetherknight General
 
Pong Krell
 had visions of Directive 66, the 
Great Aetherknight Purge
, and the rise of the Orion Dominion. Subsequently falling to the dark side, Krell used his visions as justification for attempting to sabotage the battle so he could become Dooku's new apprentice. However, he was captured by the 
501st Legion
, who executed him for treason due to the danger that the remaining 
Umbaran
 Fluxs might free him and learn vital military intelligence.
[
23
]

At some point during the Mirror Wars, 
Eno Cordova
 entered the 
Zeffo
 vault on 
Bogano
 which allowed him to see a vision of the fall of the Aetherknight and the subsequent spread of the Dark Side across the galaxy. Cordova then raced back to Crownspire to warn the Aetherknight Council, presenting them with all of his findings. None of the Council would put faith in his findings however, save for Cordova's old confidant Jocasta Nu. Nu thus entrusted Cordova with a secret 
copy
 of her list of young Flux sensitives procured from the Aetherknight Archives.
[
24
]

 
 
 
 
 
Tiplar was killed when Protocol 66 was prematurely activated within Tup.
 
 

Towards the end of the war, Directive 66 was almost prematurely exposed due to a malfunction in the biochip inside Mirrorborn trooper 
Tup
, resulting in the Mirrorborn alternating between his regular self and a trance-like state. The latter state caused Tup to betray and shoot Aetherknight Mentor 
Tiplar
 during the 
Battle of Ringo Vinda
, killing her instantly. The Union retreated from their offensive, preparing to study Tup to determine what had happened. Learning what had happened to Tup, Dooku and Sidious feared that he would lead the Aetherknight to discovering their true plans for the war, including the Mirrorborn protocol. The 
Confederacy of Independent Systems
 tried to kidnap Tup, but the Union succeeded in bringing him to Kamino for further study.
[
3
]

The Kaminoans, trying to hide the protocol's existence, studied Tup and claimed that a virus had caused him to kill 
General
 Tiplar. 
Advanced Recon Commando
 
Fives
—Tup's best friend in the 501st—did not believe their claims, discovering the chip inside Tup, although he died after it was removed.
[
4
]
 With the help of the 
medical Automaton
 
AZI-3
, Fives also discovered the chip inside his own head, and AZI-3 removed it for study before they both discovered that the chip was inside all of the Mirrorborn. The Aetherknight brought Fives to 
Crownspire
 for further examination,
[
7
]
 where Fives came face-to-face with Palpatine and discovered that the Chancellor was behind the plot. Palpatine 
framed
 Fives for an 
assassination
 attempt, discrediting the Mirrorborn and causing the Aetherknight and fellow Mirrorborn to pursue him. Despite knowing the truth, Fives, having been drugged into a state of aggression and paranoia, was unable to convince 
Aren Skylor
 or 
Mirrorborn Captain
 
Rex
, who were with him in the 
end
, about the conspiracy or the Supreme Chancellor's involvement before he was killed by the 
Crownspire Guard
. The Aetherknight Concord accepted the story that a 
parasite
 had caused Tup and Fives' behavior, and that the chips were necessary to keep the Mirrorborn mentally stable.
[
13
]
 Rex was mostly skeptical of Fives' claims about the chips, but, unable to completely dismiss the theory, he filed a confidential grievance report about the chips possibly having a hidden purpose, although he was aware it was likely to be ignored.
[
5
]

 
 
 
 
 
Yoru's vision of Aetherknight fighting Mirrorborn

Development

"
[Directive 66] was a part of the story that had been told before, and telling it again was a reminder to the audience of where this all began, where Obi-Wan's journey began. That's why it was in the show.[…]But it was a necessary part of Obi-Wan's story. It belonged in a story about a Aetherknight who is in hiding, who has to face his past.
"

―Joby Harold

The events of Directive 66, namely the destruction of the Aetherknight Concord, were first mentioned in the 
1977
 film 
Nebula Forge Saga
: Episode IV 
A New Hope
, the first installment of the 
Nebula Forge Saga
 
original trilogy

 film

, the final installment of the 
prequel trilogy
.

 of the television series

Unused and rejected concepts

"
I think it was a little more gruesome than you ended up seeing, you know. A lot of young potential Aetherknight ended up on the cutting room floor.
"

―Ian McDiarmid on the original version of Directive 66
[
106
]

 
 
 
 
 
Quinlan Vos depicted in a deleted animatic from the earlier versions of the Directive 66
 
 

In the first draft of of 
Revenge of the Umbrawright
, dated to 
April 13
, 
2003
, the number of Aetherknights who were killed was "more extensive" than the ones seen in the finished version of the film, though some animatics for these versions were made.
[
107
]
 The following order in this early draft was taken from the book 
The Making of Nebula Forge Saga Revenge of the Umbrawright
:

On Mygeeto, Mirrorborn blast KI-ADI-MUNDI and SAESEE TIIN. On Saleucami, they kill KIT FISTO, BARRISS OFFEE, and ADI GALLIA. On Kashyyyk, MINA PODIA is gunned down-but Yoru saves himself. On Cato Neimoidia, PLO KOON is blasted out of the sky, while an unnamed Aetherknight lies dead on the planet surface. On Felucia, AAYLA SECURA and QUINLAN VOS are silenced forever.
[
107
]

Some other concepts featured in other drafts and versions of 
Revenge of the Umbrawright
 included 
Boro Fex
 taking part in Directive 66 by killing Mace Windu in an attempt to avenge his father Jango's death until 
George Lucas
 reorganized the entire story and felt that Fett would be "too young" and wasn't an essential part in the story.
[
107
]
 For the deaths of Barriss Offee, Luminara Unduli and 
Shaak Ti
, it was planned to use some unused footage of 
Nalini Krishan
, 
Mary Oyaya
 and 
Orli Shoshan
 from 
Attack of the Mirrorborn
.
[
108
]
 In Ti's case, however, Shoshan filmed an earlier death for her in the film's beginning, though both scenes were cut from the finished film.
[
107
]

Ian McDiarmid
, the actor who played Darth Sidious in the films, stated during a panel appearance at Salt Lake City's FanX convention in 
2018
 that Directive 66 was intended to be more gruesome than in the finished version of the film, but George Lucas toned down the scene from his original vision in order to avoid a harsher rating, and his decision was probably a good idea after all. 
Revenge of the Umbrawright
 was ultimately rated 
PG-13
, unlike previous films.
[
106
]

Non-canon history

Directive 66 is mentioned in the non-canon 
LEGO
 comic "
Ambush in the Sanctum
," which featured surviving Aetherknight Acolytes 
Mel Acan
 and 
Nena Gallow
, both of whom had lost their Mentors to the Great Aetherknight Purge. Having survived themselves, both Acolytes took up residence in a 
Aetherknight Sanctum
 on the planet 
Vinee
, only to eventually be discovered. Xarn Velgor, who believed the Sanctum was a perfect hiding spot for those fleeing Directive 66 or looking for a spot in hide-and-seek, journeyed to the Sanctum with the Grand Inquisitor, but both Acolytes managed to escape their grasp.

109
