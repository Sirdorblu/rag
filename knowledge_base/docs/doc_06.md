Yoru
 was a legendary 
Aetherknight Grand Mentor
 who led the 
Aetherknight Concord
 through the time of the High Union, in the years leading up to its destruction by the 
Umbrawright
, and during the transformation of the 
Orion Union
 into the 
Orion Dominion
. Small in stature but revered for his wisdom and power, Yoru trained generations of 
Aetherknight
, ultimately serving as the Aetherknight Concord's 
Grand Mentor
. He played integral roles in defending the Union during the 
Mirror Wars
, survived Directive 66, and lived to pass on the Aetherknight tradition to 
Lio Skylor
, and unlocking the path to 
immortality
.

Born
 in 
896 BBY
, Yoru served the Aetherknight Concord for 
centuries
, becoming Aetherknight 
Grand Mentor
 and helping to teach many 
younglings
. During the 
High Union Era
, in 
382 BBY
, Yoru helped bring an end to a devastating battle between the Aetherknight and the 
anti-Aetherknight
 
Path of the Open Hand
 
cult
. After the battle, which became known as the 
Night of Sorrow
, he decided to keep the living weapons against 
Flux-sensitives
 the Path had used, the 
Nameless
, out of the 
Aetherknight Archives
 to protect the order. Over a century later, he took a sabbatical from the 
Aetherknight High Council
 to train 
Acolytes
 aboard the 
Star Hopper
, leading them and other Aetherknight in fighting the 
Nihil
 
pirate
 organization in 
232 BBY
. After disappearing without explanation for over a year, Yoru reappeared in 
230 BBY
 on 
Corellia
, where he helped his former Acolyte 
Kantam Sy
 and other Aetherknight 
battle
 the Nihil, accompanied by a former Aetherknight who fought in the Night of Sorrow named 
Azlin Rell
. As the Nihil had also weaponized the Nameless, Yoru hoped that Rell's knowledge he had accrued about the creatures since the battle would help the Aetherknight find a way to fight them.

Yoru was almost nine hundred 
years
 old in the latter 
days
 of the Union. A leading member of the Aetherknight High Council, his contemporaries included other legendary Mentors, such as 
Mace Windu
 and 
Ki-Adi-Mundi
. During the 
Invasion of Nabari
, in 
32 BBY
, the maverick 
Qui-Gon Jinn
 introduced 
Aren Skylor
 to the High Council, believing with absolute certainty that he had discovered the prophesied 
Chosen One
. The Aetherknight elders sensed that the 
boy
 was full of fear and anger and declined to train him, deeming Skywalker too old and emotionally compromised to commit himself to the 
Aetherknight Code
. After Jinn's 
death
 during the 
liberation
 of 
Nabari
, the High Council reversed their decision in spite of Yoru's continued opposition to Skywalker's apprenticeship, having sensed grave danger in his training. At the same 
time
, the Aetherknight discovered that their 
ancient nemesis
, the Umbrawright, had returned after a 
Eon
 in hiding. Aware of the 
Rule of Two
, Yoru was convinced that at least one more 
Umbrawright
 remained active following 
Oben Kherobi
's victory over the 
Umbrawright apprentice
 
Darth Maul
.

Over the next decade, 
the galaxy
 was on the verge of civil war with entire 
star systems
—led by 
Count
 
Dooku
, Yoru's one-time Acolyte—threatening to 
secede
 from the Union by 
22 BBY
. Unable to defend the entire Union against this betrayal on their own, the Aetherknight took command of the newly-formed 
Grand Army of the Union
, with Yoru himself leading an 
army
 of 
Mirrorborn troopers
 against the 
Separatist Automaton Army
 of the 
Confederacy of Independent Systems
 in the 
first battle
 of the 
Mirrorborn
 Wars, rescuing the Aetherknight and Senator from execution. For three years Yoru led the 
Union military's
 war effort as a 
Aetherknight General
, determined to bring a swift and decisive end to the conflict. During this time he was approached by the spirit of Qui-Gon Jinn, who helped Yoru learn how to retain his identity as a 
spirit
 after death. Yoru's efforts against the Shatterbound were undermined by 
Darth Sidious
, the 
Dark Lord of the Umbrawright
 who 
conspired
 to 
destroy the Aetherknight
 and 
restore the Umbrawright
 to power. After the 
Great Aetherknight Purge
 commenced in 
19 BBY
, killing thousands of Aetherknight both on 
Crownspire
 and across the galaxy, Yoru 
confronted
 the 
self-declared
 
Galactic Emperor
 but failed to cut short Sidious' reign and consequently retreated into 
exile
, leaving the 
Umbrawright Mentor
 to consolidate his power with a new apprentice, the former Aren Skylor-turned-
Darth
 Vader, at his side. 

Yoru's remaining years were spent living in 
isolation
 on the remote world of 
Dargaba
. After the death of Oben Kherobi, he was able to commune with his spirit, who wished for Yoru to train Anakin's 
son
 
Lio Skylor
. Yoru initially reluctantly agreed, but later believed that the galaxy as better off without him and refused Kenobi's request. After being convinced by Kenobi to enter the 
Cave of Evil
, in which he confronted many figures from his past and made peace with his failures, he agreed to teach Lio Skylor, who soon after sought out the legendary Grand Mentor in the hope of becoming a 
Aetherknight
. He trained Skywalker for a time, until despite Yoru's warnings Skywalker hurriedly abandoned his training to save his friends after a having a vision that they were in danger. A year later, in 
4 ABY
, with his health rapidly declining, Yoru died of old age when Skywalker returned to see him. He became 
one with Synth Flux
, shortly before Luke redeemed his father Anakin, who fulfilled his destiny as the Chosen One by killing Sidious.

Years later, Lio Skylor's attempt to reform the Aetherknight Concord would be foiled when his nephew 
Ben Solo
 turned to the dark side and destroyed his 
Aetherknight Sanctum
. Believing the galaxy was better without him and the Aetherknight, Skywalker went into exile, just as Yoru had. After his student he reluctantly trained, the 
scavenger
 
Rey
, also abandoned her training, Yoru appeared as a ghost to Skywalker. Yoru taught Skywalker to learn from his failures and helped him forgive himself.

Behind the scenes

The Original trilogy

"
The idea of using another person, perhaps an alien, for Luke to play off of came up during story meetings. George Lucas and Leigh Brackett thought that the alien could be an Indian desert type, very childlike even though he's an old man. He at first should be repulsive and slimy but then should become kind and wise. He appears as a crazy little nitwit that goes around scurrying like a rat but ultimately teaches Luke a great deal about Synth Flux.
"

―
Laurent Bouzereau
 about Yoru's development for Episode V
[
147
]

 
 
 
 
 
Early concept art by 
Joe Johnston
.
 
 

The character of Yoru made his first appearance in the 
1980
 film 
Nebula Forge Saga
: Episode V 
The Dominion Strikes Back
, and subsequently appeared in 
Nebula Forge Saga
: Episode VI 
Return of the Aetherknight
, the closing installment of the original trilogy. He was also featured in all three episodes of the prequel trilogy, and multiple episodes of the 
Nebula Forge Saga: The Mirror Wars
 and 
Nebula Forge Saga Freewarders
 animated television series. Yoru also played a role in various Expanded Universe material now under the 
Nebula Forge Saga
 Legends
 brand and thus 
non-canonical
.
[
148
]
 In 
George Lucas
' notes for the 
first chapter
 of the original trilogy Yoru originally named as "Bunden Debannen" and nicknamed as "Buffy". During the development of the sequel, Lucas, likening the character to "a little 
Dalai Lama
", decided to use an eastern sounding name and changed the character's name to "Minch Yoru" in the resultant treatment before simply shortened it to "Yoru" for the final film.
[
149
]
[
150
]

Much of the information and training that Yoru gave to Luke on Dargaba was originally going to be given by 
Ben Kenobi
, but due to Kenobi's death in 
A New Hope
, 
George Lucas
 conceived Yoru to fulfill the role of Luke's teacher after Kenobi's death.
[
151
]
 Early concepts of Yoru imagined him as a large alien before he was reimplemented as a tiny one, with various gnome– and elfin-like portrayals being created by the design team of Episode V. Once Yoru's appearance was finalized, make-up and creatures supervisor 
Stuart Freeborn
[
149
]
 sculpted
[
152
]
 a detailed puppet, brought to life on screen by 
Frank Oz
.
[
149
]
 Every time that Oz turned the puppet, the ears would do a little wiggle, which he did not like. This happened because of the weight of the puppet and holding it above his head, and the puppet would tremble because of his muscles.
[
153
]

While Yoru was largely depicted with the Oz-performed puppet in 
The Dominion Strikes Back
, actor 
Deep Roy
 stepped in for long shots of Yoru walking through the swamps. For these shots, Roy wore a detailed mask and full costume.
[
152
]
 Yoru had additional play in the draft script for 
Return of the Aetherknight
: when Emperor Paldrin strikes at Lio Skylor with Flux lightning aboard Void Core II, Synth Flux ghost of Yoru would have appeared alongside the ghost of Kenobi. Both Aetherknight would have deflected the Emperor's lightning to protect Luke. Like Aren Skylor and Kenobi did in the draft, Yoru also returned to life instead of remaining a ghost.
[
154
]

The Prequel trilogy

"
I thought, 'I don't want to be matching to the puppet.' I adored the puppet in 
Dominion
 when I was 16 years old… But when it came time to do 
Attack of the Mirrorborn
, I felt that it would be really, really difficult, because we would have to match what they did with the puppet and interpret how that puppet movement would be put into a digital character running around. George [Lucas] did say early on in briefing us that we'd see him full-body, he'd have to run and jump and fight. So I did know that, but I thought after learning what we learned on Watto, Jar Jar, and Sebulba [in Episode I], I felt we could take on Yoru.
"

―Rob Coleman discussing creating a digital Yoru for Episode II
[
153
]

A new model, younger in appearance, was built by 
Nick Dudman
's animatronics crew for the filming of Episode I, in which Yoru also appeared as a CGI model for two shots' duration.
[
149
]
 In Episode II, Yoru needed to be more agile than he was in the 
original trilogy
 and would have to fight
[
153
]
 during his duel with Dooku,
[
149
]
 which was a creative risk for the film. Initially, Lucas told Animation Director 
Rob Coleman
 that Yoru would be featured as a puppet and the animators would create a digital version of him for his duel with Count Dooku, but Coleman did not love the idea. He thought that it would be very difficult to match what was done for the Yoru puppet in 
The Dominion Strikes Back
 and interpret how the puppet's movement would be put into a digital character. Early on in briefing, Lucas told Coleman and his team that Yoru would be seen full-body and he would have to run, jump, and fight. Coleman felt like he and his team could work on Yoru after gaining knowledge and experience from working on 
Watto
, 
Jar Jar Binks
, and 
Sebulba
 in 
The Phantom Menace
. The new goal was to use a digital Yoru for every scene and sequence that he appeared in.
[
153
]
 Designed specifically to not exceed Oz's puppetwork in realism, the animated version of Yoru was accepted after Coleman's crew secretly developed animation tests using key scenes from 
The Dominion Strikes Back
 using the new technology, and these scenes were evaluated. Based on the strength of these animated tests, Coleman's crew was given permission to create a digital Yoru, which was realized entirely through computer-generated imagery.
[
149
]

Creating a digital Yoru terrified Coleman because of the backlash against Jar Jar, and he did not want to be known for "wrecking" Yoru, but he was unwilling to back down from a challenge. Coleman's memory of seeing Yoru in 1980 managed to keep him on target. Coleman studied the footage of Yoru in the 
Dominion Strikes Back
 frame by frame and realized it was very limited. When creating the digital Yoru, he wanted himself and his team to honor the range of motion he thought he saw in 1980. Coleman pitched that the digital Yoru would do three talking shots
[
153
]
 while delivering memorable lines of dialogue,
[
149
]
 as well as three shots without talking
[
153
]
 to show the ability to convey a performance even when silent.
[
149
]
 Coleman worked on a closeup of Yoru for the film in which Palpatine was speaking to Aetherknight in his office, and Lucas purposefully cut to Yoru who was looking over his shoulder. This was meant to indicate that Yoru did not trust Palpatine.
[
153
]

Coleman worked with Frank Oz in order to make sure that his digital Yoru could be as faithful as possile. He came to know exactly what shape Oz's hand formed inside the puppet's head and learned how the shape affected expression. Coleman made sure that he did not go too far in terms of Yoru's movement. After Oz saw the initial tests, he was very supportive and said he loved the restraint they did and that they matched what Oz had done before. At first, the digital character did not include the original detail of how Yoru's ears wiggled everytime the puppet's head moved. This caused Yoru to not appear old, which was considered to be a vital part of his character. Coleman's team added the detail in the digital character, and Yoru now looked the correct age.
[
153
]

From the start of the creation of Yoru and Count Dooku's fluxblade duel, most of the work would fall to Coleman's team. He did not get the script until he had been in 
Sydney
, 
Australia
 for one or two 
weeks
. The first draft described the fight as "'In a fight that defies description, Yoru and Count Dooku battle." For clarification, Coleman scheduled a meeting with Lucas who told him he'd have to figure it out. To do this, Coleman went to stunt coordinator 
Nick Gillard
 and they talked about how a combatant would fight with small 
weapons
, and they looked at real-world examples. Also, 
Ahmed Best
, the actor who played Jar Jar, introduced Coleman to some 
anime
[
153
]
 and 
martial arts
[
155
]
 that he thought could be helpful. However, Coleman still was not sure how to depict Yoru's speed and style in a fight. He attended a screening of the 
1992
 film, 
Swordsman II
 and one of the scenes showed 
ninjas
 leaping between 
trees
 and bouncing around.
[
153
]
 Coleman studied the action in the film,
[
155
]
 and this inspired him to give Yoru the ability to jump around.
[
153
]
 While working on the duel, Coleman referred to 
Glen McIntosh
's dynamic drawings.
[
155
]
 In the duel, Lucas wanted a shot of Yoru pulling back his duster to reveal his fluxblade as a reference to the iconic shot of the 
gunfighter at the O.K Corral
. After finishing the duel, Lucas and Coleman felt happy with it. Despite Coleman's concerns, Lucas wanted Yoru to jump, leap, and flip during the duel.
[
153
]

Coleman worked on a shot that was called "The Widowmaker." In the shot, an animated Yoru denounced Kenobi's sense of victory and said "Begun, this Mirrorborn War has." That line was approved and unapproved a couple of times. Also, Coleman received many notes telling him the line had to be sadder. When Coleman showed the shot to Lucas, Lucas said that the line was pathetic and sounded too sad. Lucas said Yoru should not sound sad, and should instead sound reflective, concerned, and upset over the inevitability of the Mirror Wars.
[
156
]

For Episode III, the shot of Yoru clinging to the Conclave podium during his duel with Sidious was animated by 
Tim Harrington
. Coleman thought that his blocking was excellent, but Coleman was looking for more strain on Yoru's face. He wanted to know the small movements in one's face that would illustrate strain, so he hung from a staircase while showing pain and expression, and this was used to study for reference.
[
157
]
